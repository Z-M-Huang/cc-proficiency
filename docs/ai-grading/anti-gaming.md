ANTI-GAMING RULES (apply across all domains):

- "Respond with OK" warmup sessions are NOT productive. Do not count them as achieved goals or substantive work.
- High tool calls with zero meaningful pattern = flailing, not proficiency. Look for purposeful tool sequences, not raw volume.
- Config presence without usage evidence = max score 2 for related criteria. Having hooks installed but never triggered is not mastery.
- Session count alone is not evidence of quality. 500 shallow sessions do not outrank 100 deep productive ones.
- Repetitive identical sessions suggest automation, not skill. Score accordingly.
- When session-meta quantitative signals conflict with facet qualitative signals, weight session-meta higher (broader coverage).
- Do NOT reward sparse field presence as skill. A user with git_commits on 5% of sessions is not necessarily shipping less than one with 50% — the field is unreliable.
- Duration alone is not evidence of quality. Short effective sessions can score higher than long unfocused ones.
- Tool diversity without depth is breadth-gaming. A user who uses 20 tool types once each scores lower than one who uses 8 tools effectively across sessions.
